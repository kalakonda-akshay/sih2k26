01

Refined product vision

The problem statement asks for a logistics dashboard. The winning version of it is an accessibility graph of the North East that is always current — and every module is a different question asked of that one graph.

Urban logistics platforms optimise across thousands of redundant paths, so the interesting work is cost minimisation. NER inverts this. Between Guwahati and, say, Champhai there are effectively two viable corridors. When NH‑306 fails at a single cutting, the alternative is not five percent longer — it is nine hours longer, or it does not exist. The scarce resource is not fuel. It is connectivity itself.

So the atomic unit of the system is the road segment, and the primary intelligence is segment survivability: will this piece of road still be passable in 24 and 72 hours, and if not, which districts fall off the network? A platform built on that unit answers the district-collector question that no commercial navigation app can: “If Sonapur closes tomorrow, which blocks lose their medicine route, and what is the fallback?”

Four continuous questions

Predict

Will it stay open?

Calibrated 0–100 disruption probability per segment at 24 h and 72 h horizons, with a signed contribution for every input feature.

Navigate

What is the best way?

Routing over a graph whose edge costs are risk-adjusted travel times, producing Fastest, Safest and Emergency variants that genuinely differ.

Deliver

Where is the load?

Consignment-level tracking with ETA-at-risk detection and automatic replan the moment an edge on the active route degrades.

Prove

Why did it say that?

Every score, alert and reroute carries provenance and evidence. In a government system, auditability beats magic.

The differentiating capability

District reachability under predicted failure. Given the current risk surface, compute for every district whether a supply depot is still reachable, and the marginal cost of the best remaining path. Render it as a choropleth that goes amber and then red before the road actually fails. No commercial product does this, it is directly what MDoNER needs, and it is a single graph query on infrastructure we are already building for routing.

Who it is for

Administrator

MDoNER · State PWD

Region-wide picture, high-risk corridor register, user and role management, incident verification queue, policy-grade analytics and export.

Logistics operator

Transporter · FCI · CMSS

Fleet and consignment registration, live tracking, disruption alerts on active routes, one-click acceptance of a recommended reroute.

Field officer

Block officer · BRO · NGO

Offline-first geo-tagged incident capture with photographs, road accessibility updates, personal sync queue. Phone-shaped, low bandwidth.

Emergency authority

SDMA · NDRF · DC office

Emergency mode, isolated-district list, accessible-corridor computation, priority allocation of medical and relief loads, escalation trail.

02

System architecture

A modular monolith — one FastAPI process containing eight bounded service modules with separate schemas and no cross-module imports except through interfaces. Deployable as microservices later; buildable by six people in a hackathon window now.

Three architectural commitments

1 · Risk is precomputed on segments, never per request

A scheduled job scores every segment every fifteen minutes (and on demand when an incident lands), writing to segment_risk_current with the feature vector and contributions attached. Routing, the map, analytics and alerts all read that cached surface. This keeps route planning under 300 ms, makes every score reproducible after the fact, and means the ML model is never on the critical path of a user request.

2 · Everything interesting is an event

Domain events — incident.verified, risk.band_changed, segment.blocked, route.invalidated, vehicle.entered_high_risk, consignment.eta_slipped — are published to Redis. Alerts, WebSocket fan-out, analytics counters and the audit log are all subscribers. Adding a new reaction later means adding a subscriber, not editing the incident handler.

3 · The demo runs on a scenario clock

A ScenarioRunner replays a YAML timeline against the feed adapter in replay mode — rainfall rises, forecasts update, the simulator ticks, the incident is injected. Judges get a control bar with play, pause, reset and jump-to-step. Rehearsable fifty times, identical every time, no network dependency in the room.

06

AI / ML architecture

The instruction was explicit: no fake AI labels. So the risk engine is three named layers, each of which emits its own delta into the explain payload. A judge can point at any number on screen and be told exactly where it came from.

Model A — segment disruption risk

PropertyChoiceWhy this and not the obvious alternative
TaskBinary classification, P(disruption ≤ H)Regression on “risk” has no ground truth. A closure either happened or it did not.
AlgorithmHistGradientBoostingClassifierHandles missing features natively (feeds do fail), trains in seconds, and TreeSHAP is exact on it. Stays inside the specified scikit-learn stack.
CalibrationIsotonic regression, held-out foldRaw GBT scores are not probabilities. Without this, “87/100” means nothing and the claim is indefensible.
Primary metricPR-AUC and Brier scoreDisruptions are ~2–4 % of segment-days. ROC-AUC flatters imbalanced problems; quoting PR-AUC signals you know that.
Operating metricRecall at top 10 % of segmentsThe real question is “if a control room can watch 60 segments today, how many of tomorrow's failures are in that 60?”
ExplainabilityTreeSHAP, cached with the scoreComputed once per scoring pass, not per request. The API just reads it.

The feature that will impress

Antecedent Precipitation Index — a 14-day exponentially weighted sum of prior rainfall, APIₜ = k·APIₜ₋₁ + Pₜ with k ≈ 0.9. Rainfall-triggered landslides are governed by soil saturation, not by today's rain alone; a 40 mm day after a dry fortnight is far less dangerous than 40 mm on already-saturated slopes. It is three lines of pandas, it is genuine slope-stability science, and it is the difference between a model that has read the literature and a model that has read a tutorial.

Models B and C

Model B

Hazard-type classifier

Multinomial GBT over the same feature vector, conditioned on at-risk segments. Outputs a probability distribution over landslide, flooding, washout, bridge failure and snow blockage — which is what populates “Predicted Issue: Possible Landslide” rather than a hard-coded string.

Model C

Segment travel-time regressor

Gradient boosting on observed traversal times given road class, gradient, surface, rainfall, hour of day and vehicle type. Feeds the routing edge weights and powers ETA-at-risk detection — the alert that fires when a consignment's predicted arrival slips past its promise.

Training data — and being honest about it

There is no public per-segment closure dataset for the North East. Pretending otherwise is the fastest way to lose credibility with a technical judge. The defensible approach, which should be stated openly in the presentation:

  1. Real inputs. Terrain, slope and elevation are derived from actual SRTM/Cartosat DEM. The road network is real OSM geometry. Susceptibility comes from published Bhuvan/GSI landslide zonation. Historical rainfall is real IMD gridded data. None of this is invented.
  2. Seeded real labels. A few hundred genuine closure events compiled from NDMA and state SDMA bulletins, BRO advisories and news archives for the 2019–2024 monsoons.
  3. Physically-grounded augmentation. Additional labels generated by a documented hazard function over real rainfall and real terrain — not by a random number generator. The generator is committed to the repository and shown on request.
  4. A model card that says all of this, served at GET /risk/model and linked from the UI.
Judge-proofing

“How did you train this?” is the single most likely hard question you will face. Rehearse a 30-second answer that names real inputs, admits the label augmentation, explains the physical basis of the generator, and points at the model card. Volunteering the limitation reads as rigour; being caught hiding it reads as fabrication.

Risk bands

Safe 0–24 Moderate 25–49 High 50–74 Critical 75–100

Confidence is reported separately from score, as Low / Medium / High with its reason — derived from feature freshness (how many inputs are stale beyond six hours), calibration bin width at that probability, and prediction spread across the ensemble. “High risk, low confidence, because rainfall telemetry for this grid cell is nine hours old” is an operationally useful sentence that almost no competing prototype will produce.

07

GIS architecture

The map is the product. It must render a few thousand risk-coloured segments smoothly, work without a network connection in the field app, and look like a command centre rather than a hobby project.

Data acquisition and preprocessing

A one-time pipeline in data/pipeline/, checkpointed at every stage so a failure does not cost the whole run:

  1. Extract — Geofabrik Northeast India OSM extract; filter with osmium to motorway | trunk | primary | secondary | tertiary plus district-connector roads. Expect 15–25 k ways before simplification.
  2. Load and node — osm2pgsql into PostGIS, then pgr_createTopology to build road_nodes and populate source/target on every segment.
  3. Simplify — ST_SimplifyPreserveTopology at rendering tolerance for the tile layer; keep full geometry for routing. Target roughly 1,200 segments in the demo working set.
  4. Terrain enrich — sample SRTM 30 m DEM with rasterio zonal statistics along each segment: mean and max slope, elevation gain, HAND (height above nearest drainage). This is the step that takes the longest — start it on day one.
  5. Hazard join — spatial join against Bhuvan landslide susceptibility and CWC flood hazard polygons; write class and index onto each segment.
  6. Chokepoint extraction — OSM bridge=yes, tunnel=yes, ford=yes tags plus manual annotation of the corridors that actually matter for the demo.
  7. Seed — districts from GADM/Survey of India, depots from real facility locations, vehicles and users generated.

Routing engine

pgRouting over mv_routing_edges, a materialised view that carries three cost columns recomputed on every risk pass. The variants are genuinely different objectives, not the same path relabelled:

-- FASTEST: pure predicted travel time
cost_fastest  = length_m / speed_effective(road_class, surface, gradient, rain)

-- SAFEST: time penalised by risk, with chokepoints weighted by criticality
cost_safest   = cost_fastest × (1 + α × risk_score / 100)
              + β × Σ chokepoint_criticality
                                          -- α = 1.8, β = 120s per criticality point

-- EMERGENCY: safest, plus hard feasibility constraints
cost_emergency = cost_safest
   WHERE risk_score < 75                       -- never route relief into a critical segment
     AND status = 'OPEN'
     AND max_gvw_t ≥ vehicle.gvw_t             -- the bridge must actually take the load
     AND min_chokepoint_capacity ≥ vehicle.gvw_t

Blocked segments are excluded from the view entirely, so pgr_bdAstar simply cannot return them — automatic rerouting is a consequence of the data model rather than a special case in application code. That is the cleanest possible answer to “how does it recalculate?” Alternatives come from pgr_ksp (K-shortest paths) with a similarity filter so the three options presented are meaningfully distinct rather than three variants of the same road.

Recommended change from the brief

Use MapLibre GL JS with PMTiles rather than Leaflet. Three reasons, in order of importance: it renders several thousand styled line features on the GPU where Leaflet's SVG layer stalls; a single PMTiles file gives the field PWA a genuinely offline basemap, which the offline requirement otherwise cannot fully satisfy; and terrain extrusion turns the region's topography into something a judge can see rather than infer. It is open source with no token and no billing. If the team has never touched MapLibre, Leaflet with a canvas renderer is an acceptable fallback — but decide this in phase 1, not phase 6.

08

Offline behaviour & language

The field officer is the user with the worst connectivity and the most valuable data. Offline is not a feature flag on that interface — it is the default assumption.

The write path when there is no network

  1. The form writes to IndexedDB (Dexie, store pendingIncidents) with a client_uuid from crypto.randomUUID() and a device_ts. The photograph is compressed client-side to WebP under 1 MB and stored as a Blob in mediaBlobs.
  2. The UI updates optimistically: the incident appears on the officer's local map with a Pending sync chip. No spinner, no error — from the officer's point of view the report is filed.
  3. A Background Sync registration (sync-incidents) is queued. If Background Sync is unavailable, an online event listener plus a 60-second poll covers the gap.
  4. On reconnection the service worker drains the queue to POST /incidents/sync as a single batch, in device_ts order.
  5. The server deduplicates on client_uuid against sync_receipts and returns a per-item resolution — created, duplicate or rejected. Replays are therefore free, which matters because flaky links produce them constantly.
  6. The client reconciles: purge the accepted, mark the rejected with a readable reason, retry the failed with exponential backoff.

Conflict resolution is trivially safe here because incidents are append-only observations, not mutable records. The server timestamp is authoritative for ordering; device_ts is preserved so a report filed at 06:10 and synced at 14:30 still reads as a 06:10 observation.

What is cached for offline reading

Precached

App shell

Routes, components, icons and both translation bundles, versioned by build hash.

On assignment

District map slice

PMTiles subset plus segment geometry for the officer's assigned district, in Cache Storage. Roughly 8–20 MB.

Last known

Risk snapshot

Latest risk bands with an explicit “as of HH:MM” stamp, so stale data is never mistaken for live data.

Multilingual

next-intl with locale-prefixed routes (/en, /hi), messages split into per-module namespaces so a new language is a directory of JSON files and one entry in the locale registry — no code changes. Two details that matter beyond the UI strings:

  • Alerts are bilingual at rest. The alerts table stores title_en/title_hi and body_en/body_hi, generated from templates at creation time. An alert delivered to a Hindi-locale user is Hindi in the notification, in the export and in the audit record — not translated client-side at display time.
  • Number, date and unit formatting go through Intl with the active locale, including the Indian digit grouping convention.

Assamese, Manipuri, Khasi and Mizo are structurally supported from day one and listed in Settings as “coming soon” — which is honest, and demonstrates that the architecture anticipated them.

The demo cascade

The ten-step scenario in the brief is not a demo script bolted on at the end. It is a specification for the event spine, and it should be treated as a build target from phase 5 onward.

The scenario file

A YAML timeline replayed by the runner against a virtual clock, so a 72-hour meteorological build-up compresses into a four-minute demonstration:

scenario: ri_bhoi_landslide
clock: { start: "2026-06-18T02:00+05:30", speed: 1080 }   # 18h per minute
steps:
  - at: T+0h    inject: rainfall   { grid: RI-BHOI-*, rain_24h: 45 }
  - at: T+6h    inject: rainfall   { grid: RI-BHOI-*, rain_24h: 118, warning: ORANGE }
  - at: T+6h    expect: risk_band  { segment: NH6-KH-014, band: HIGH }
  - at: T+9h    inject: forecast   { horizon: 24, rain_mm: 160, warning: RED }
  - at: T+9h    expect: alert      { type: LANDSLIDE_RISK, severity: HIGH }
  - at: T+11h   inject: incident   { by: officer_kh_02, type: LANDSLIDE,
                                     severity: CRITICAL, at: [91.88, 25.74] }
  - at: T+11h   expect: segment    { id: NH6-KH-014, status: BLOCKED }
  - at: T+11h   expect: reroute    { consignments: [MED-2291, FOOD-1180] }

The expect assertions are not decoration — they run in CI as an end-to-end integration test. If a refactor breaks the cascade, the build fails rather than the demonstration.

Development plan

Eight phases, ordered by dependency rather than by the module numbering in the brief. Each has an exit criterion that is demonstrable — if you cannot show it, the phase is not finished and you do not start the next one.

The ordering principle

Build the data spine before any feature: segments in the database, risk on the segments, events on the wire. Six of the eight modules are readers of that spine. Every day spent building a feature before the spine exists is a day of rework, and rework is what kills hackathon teams in the final week.

~2 days · whole team · no external dependencies

Monorepo, docker compose with Postgres + PostGIS + pgRouting + Redis + MinIO, Alembic baseline, FastAPI skeleton with health check and OpenAPI, Next.js shell with the design tokens and dark theme, JWT auth end to end, four seeded demo accounts, role-guarded routes, CI running lint, typecheck and tests.

Approval gate

Five decisions are yours, not mine. Answer these and I will start building from phase 00 — complete, integrated, production-quality code, one module at a time, in the order above.

1 · MapLibre or Leaflet?

This is the one call that is expensive to reverse, because the entire map component tree depends on it.

Recommended: MapLibre GL — unless nobody on the team has used it, in which case Leaflet with a canvas renderer is honest and safe.
2 · How much time do you actually have?

The plan totals roughly 29 working days at one owner per stream. Tell me your real deadline and team size and I will compress the phases, most likely by narrowing the geographic scope to three states instead of eight for the road network while keeping all eight rendered at district level.

3 · Do you want the multi-modal river and rail extension?

It is the strongest NER-specific differentiator on the advanced list — Brahmaputra ferry legs are genuinely how supplies move — but it adds a node type, an edge type and a schedule model to the routing engine.

Recommended: defer to a stretch phase, but design the schema for it now so the slide can honestly say “the graph already supports it”.
4 · Real data feeds, or replay only?

Live IMD or OpenWeather integration is roughly half a day and gives you a real answer to “is this live?”. Replay-only is safer in the room. The adapter supports both, so this is a question about where to spend half a day.

Recommended: build both, demo in hybrid — live weather, replayed scenario events.
5 · Any changes to the architecture above?

Corrections, additions, or anything you want cut. Once you approve, I start with phase 00 — monorepo, Docker compose, database with PostGIS, migrations, JWT authentication, and the application shell — and I will show you every file as it is created.
